2. When we're the controller, and when we're your processor

If we host or maintain your website, two different roles apply and it matters which is which.

Our own records about you — we're the controller

Your enquiry, your contact details, our correspondence, your invoices. We decide how and why those are handled, so this policy governs them.

Your website's data — you're the controller, we're your processor

The enquiries, orders, bookings, comments and accounts belonging to your website's visitors are yours. You decide what happens to them. We store and process them only on your instructions, keep them secure, and never use them for our own purposes. The processor obligations we owe you are set out in full in section 18 of our terms of service, which forms our written contract under Article 28 of the UK GDPR.

Your own website needs its own privacy policy covering the people who use it. That's your responsibility as controller, and we're happy to help you draft one.

3. What we collect, why, and our lawful basis

  • Enquiry details — your name, email address, organisation, existing website and whatever you write in your message. Used to reply to you and work out whether we can help. Lawful basis: legitimate interests (responding to people who ask us to get in touch), and taking steps at your request before entering a contract.
  • Client account information — contact details, billing address, plan details and payment records needed to set you up, invoice you and manage your services. Lawful basis: performance of our contract with you, and legal obligation for tax and accounting records.
  • Correspondence — emails between us, kept so we have an accurate record of what was agreed, asked for and delivered. Lawful basis: legitimate interests (keeping a reliable record of the work and being able to defend a dispute).
  • Website admin access — the login accounts we hold or issue for your website, hosting and domain. Lawful basis: performance of our contract with you.
  • Technical logs — standard server and security logs, including IP addresses, generated when you visit this website or a website we host. Used to keep services secure, diagnose faults and identify abuse. Lawful basis: legitimate interests (network and information security).

Where we rely on legitimate interests, we've considered whether our interest is overridden by your rights, and we think it isn't — the processing is limited, expected, and necessary to run the service. You can object at any time (see section 10).

You don't have to give us any of this. But if you don't give us your contact details we can't reply to you, and if you don't give us billing details we can't invoice you or provide the service.

4. Where the information comes from

Almost always from you directly — the contact form, an email, or a conversation. Technical logs are generated automatically when a device connects to a server we run. Occasionally we'll look at publicly available information, such as your existing website or a public company record, to prepare a quote.

Uptime and security monitoring runs on our own server, not a third-party monitoring service.

We don't sell, rent or trade personal data, and we don't share it for anyone else's marketing. We'll only disclose data beyond the list above where the law requires it, or where we need to establish or defend a legal claim.

If we change a supplier that handles client website data, we'll tell affected clients in advance and you can object. If you do object and we can't find a workable alternative, either of us can end the affected service without penalty.

6. Where data is stored, and international transfers

Websites we host currently run on servers in Germany. Backups are encrypted and stored in the EU. The EU is covered by UK adequacy regulations, so transfers there need no additional safeguards. If hosting moves to the UK, UK storage needs no additional mechanism. We give 30 days notice before any move, with updated location and energy information.

Some suppliers, such as Cloudflare and Stripe, operate global networks and may process limited data outside the UK and EU. Where they do, transfers are covered by recognised UK GDPR transfer mechanisms — an adequacy decision, or the International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, together with a transfer risk assessment where one is needed.

7. How long we keep things

  • Enquiries that don't become work — deleted within 12 months.
  • Client records, contracts and invoices — kept for as long as you're a client and then 6 years, which is what UK tax law and the limitation period require.
  • Correspondence — kept for the duration of the relationship plus 6 years, for the same reason.
  • Server and security logs — rotated automatically, typically within 30 days.
  • Backups — rotated automatically on a fixed schedule, with older copies overwritten. Deleted data may persist in a backup until that copy rotates out.
  • Website data when you leave — we hand over a full export, then delete our copies once you confirm the migration has worked, apart from the invoice records we're obliged to keep.

8. How we keep it secure

  • Encryption in transit — HTTPS on every site we run — and encrypted backups at rest.
  • Access limited to Luke and Ralph, protected by strong unique credentials, a password manager and two-factor authentication.
  • Firewalls, malware scanning and continuous monitoring on every server we manage.
  • Daily off-site backups so data can be recovered after an incident.
  • Software kept patched, and access removed promptly when it's no longer needed.

No system is perfectly secure, and we won't pretend otherwise. What we can promise is that these measures are actually in place and actually maintained.

9. Cookies, analytics and marketing

This website sets no advertising or tracking cookies and runs no analytics that identify you. It does load content delivery and font files from third party networks to display correctly, which may process IP addresses as strictly necessary for that display. If we add anything that needs consent under the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations, we will ask first and update this policy.

We do not run a newsletter and we do not send marketing emails to prospects. If we ever send marketing to existing clients about our own similar services, each message will carry an unsubscribe link and a clear opt out at collection.

10. Your rights

Under the UK GDPR you have the right to ask us to:

  • Access — give you a copy of the personal data we hold about you.
  • Rectify — correct anything inaccurate or incomplete.
  • Erase — delete data where we no longer have a good reason to keep it.
  • Restrict — pause how we use it while a question about it is resolved.
  • Port — provide the data you gave us in a common machine-readable format, or send it to another provider.
  • Object — stop processing based on legitimate interests, including any direct marketing, which we'll always stop on request.

Email hello@lrweb.uk and say what you want. We'll respond within one month, and usually a great deal faster. There's no charge unless a request is manifestly unfounded or excessive. If we can't be confident who you are, we'll ask for enough information to check before we hand anything over.

If your request concerns data on a website we host for one of our clients, we'll pass it to that client, because they're the controller for it and the decision is theirs.

11. Automated decision-making

We don't make any decisions about you by automated means, and we don't profile anyone. Quotes and decisions are made by Luke or Ralph, reading what you sent.

Our services are sold to businesses and organisations, and this website isn't aimed at children. We don't knowingly collect personal data from anyone under 13. If a website we build for you will collect data from children — a school, a club, a youth group — tell us at the start, because it changes what that site needs to do, and the responsibility for that data sits with you as controller.

13. If something goes wrong

If a breach affects your personal data, we will tell you promptly: what happened, what was affected, and what we are doing about it. Where a breach is likely to result in a risk to people's rights and freedoms, we will report it to the ICO within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, as the law requires. Where we are acting as your processor, we will notify you without undue delay and within 24 hours where practicable, with the nature, categories, likely consequences and measures taken, and preserve relevant evidence.

14. Complaints

If you're unhappy with how we've handled your data, tell us first at hello@lrweb.uk and we'll try to put it right. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at any time, at ico.org.uk or on 0303 123 1113. Complaining to us first doesn't affect that right.
